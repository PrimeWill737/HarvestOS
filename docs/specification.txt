THE MASTER BLUEPRINT
HarvestOS is a coordination and decision-intelligence operating system for perishable
agricultural produce. The platform helps farmers, buyers, storage operators, transporters
and coordinators act before market-access failures turn harvests into financial and physical
losses.
Core product principle: Build a dependable system of record first, a transparent intelligence layer second, and
executable coordination workflows third. A recommendation is not a successful transaction; an accepted offer is
not rescued food; only verified outcomes can support impact claims.

01 | Business and product strategy
1.1 Vision, mission and positioning
Vision: Agricultural supply chains in which valuable harvests are discovered, allocated and transported before
avoidable delays destroy value. Mission: Give smallholder farmers and agricultural businesses a reliable shared
operational layer for crop supply, buying, storage and distribution.
Positioning: HarvestOS differs from a simple product listing marketplace by identifying at-risk quantities,
evaluating feasible alternatives and coordinating real-world follow-through. Initial geography: Plateau State.
Initial produce: tomatoes. Subsequent crops: Irish potatoes, cabbage, pepper and others after process validation.

4.1 Supabase Session Pooler
Obtain the actual connection string from Supabase Dashboard → Connect → Session pooler. Shared session-mode
pooler typically uses port 5432; transaction-mode shared pooler typically uses port 6543. Do not assume a
sample hostname is correct. Session-mode connections stay associated with a client; small connection pools
must be sized and benchmarked for serverless concurrency.
DATABASE_URL="postgresql://postgres.PROJECT_REF:PASSWORD@POOLER_HOST:5432/postgres"
NEXT_PUBLIC_SUPABASE_URL="https://PROJECT.supabase.co"

CLOUDINARY_API_KEY="..."        # private, optional
CLOUDINARY_API_SECRET="..."     # private, optional

for tables exposed via Supabase browser/client APIs. Database passwords and privileged keys must never be
prefixed NEXT_PUBLIC_.

4.3 Module boundaries
Separate business logic from React pages. Use feature modules with validation schemas, repositories, services,
server actions, API handlers, test fixtures and role checks. Keep SQL migrations version controlled. For large
operations, asynchronous notifications should be processed from an outbox instead of inside the transaction that

05 | Functional module requirements
5.1 Public site and marketing
 • Present a short agricultural problem narrative, operating process and verified benefits by stakeholder type.
 • Separate farmers, buyers, transporters and storage-provider signup entry points.
 • Explain how recommendations work and label all simulated or pilot statistics explicitly.
 • Provide contact/privacy/terms pages and accessible account recovery.
 
5.2 Farmer workspace
 • Manage profile, organization membership, one or more farms and approximate locations.
 • Create draft harvest with crop, variety, grade, photos, dates, estimated quantity, unit and target price.
 • Publish only after validation; support updates with versioned event history.
 • Maintain multiple harvest lots and partial quantity allocation; clearly distinguish available, reserved, sold and lost
 stock.
• View ranked buyer demand, offer amounts, logistics estimates and explanations.
 • Accept/reject/counter offers with deadlines; monitor collection, delivery and settlement records.
 • Submit end-of-harvest outcome reports, inspections and disputes.
 
5.3 Buyer workspace
 • Business details and verification status; multiple delivery destinations where authorized.
 • Purchase requirements with crop, grade, quantity, maximum price, collection window, location and expiry.
 • Browse compatible farm lots; submit and track offers; confirm purchase terms.
 • Confirm received quantity and grade; flag discrepancies; attach evidence.
 • Record payment references and review history, but do not self-confirm payment without trusted verification.

5.4 Transport provider
 • Register vehicle capacity, service area, suitability (open/covered/insulated/refrigerated) and availability.
 • Quote pickup/delivery jobs; accept assignments; record dispatched, collected, in-transit, delivered and failed
 statuses.
• Provide delivery evidence, actual charges, completion time and incident reasons.
 • MVP may use coordinator-assisted bookings; full real-time vehicle tracking is explicitly out of scope.
 
5.5 Storage provider
 • Register facility, usable capacity, compatible produce, conditions, prices and available date ranges.
 • Issue and accept timed reservations; prevent capacity overbooking across overlapping periods.
 • Track goods received/released and incidents, including deterioration, wrong quantity and late collection.
 • Require a provider confirmation before displaying capacity as reserved.
 
5.6 Coordinator and administrator
 • Risk queue with explainable priority, last evaluated time and recommended interventions.
 • Review matches, generate quote requests, monitor delayed pickups and resolve unfulfilled orders.
 • Verify organizations and providers; constrain staff actions to assigned scopes; track before/after changes.
 • Configure catalogs, units, policies, score thresholds and escalation rules with versioned changes.
 • Review impact metrics and distinguish forecasted outcomes from independently verified outcomes.

6.2 Partial fulfillment and reversal
Example: 100 kg of tomatoes; Buyer A reserves 30 kg and Buyer B reserves 40 kg; 30 kg remains available. A
competing request must not reserve more than the available balance. If Buyer B cancels before dispatch under
the applicable rules, 40 kg may return to available stock; if dispatched, a controlled resolution process is
required.
6.3 Exception handling
 • Late pickups create operational incidents and trigger review.
 • Produce deterioration and rejected quantities require inspection evidence or an explicit unverified declaration.
 • Payment delays, order cancellation, duplicated retries and no-shows retain complete audit history.
 • Reservations expire safely when time windows lapse; no background task may silently double-sell inventory.
 
07 | Decision-intelligence engine
7.1 Risk scoring v1: transparent rule-based model
Prototype formula: R = 0.30U + 0.25T + 0.20S + 0.15D + 0.10C. Every factor is normalized to a 0-100 severity
value. U = uncommitted supply, T = urgency relative to crop handling time, S = storage inadequacy, D =
expected logistics delay and C = reported condition risk. Proposed—not scientifically calibrated—weights.
FACTOR
INPUT / NORMALIZATION
WEIGHT

30%

Crop-specific window, harvest timing and elapsed time
25%

20%
Delivery delay (D)

15%
Produce condition (C)
Reported/inspected grade and condition deterioration
10%
Initial bands: 0–34 low; 35–64 moderate; 65–79 high; 80–100 critical. A pre-harvest score is a
commercial-coordination urgency indicator, not a probability that produce will spoil. Keep crop handling
assumptions versioned and explicitly inspectable.

Step 2: Rank with explanations. Return buyer identity/status, requested crop and quantity, location, offer,

7.3 Fulfillment option evaluation
Net return = expected sales revenue − transport expense − storage expense − other documented transaction
costs. Compare immediate local sale, higher-price distant sale, validated temporary storage, joint transport and
alternative processing pathways. State unavailable pricing inputs, time constraints and risks; prioritize feasible
alternatives, not merely the highest gross selling price.
7.4 AI usage boundaries
Generative AI may later explain scores, translate instructions, summarize market documents or triage anomalies.
It must not autonomously commit stock, confirm payments, book services or infer scientific spoilage certainty.
The hackathon prototype is fully viable with deterministic algorithms and validated business logic.

 • Use timestamptz for events and explicit timezone treatment for local dates.

16 | International product strategy
The international opportunity is a repeatable decision infrastructure for fragmented agricultural systems, rather
than a claim that food marketplaces or cold-storage booking are new. Test measurable improvements in
transaction completion, actual loss and verified income before making cross-border impact claims.
 • Support localized crops, grades, handling windows, quality rules and farming practices.
 • Model currency, date/time and unit conversions explicitly; preserve original local units.
 • Apply country-specific privacy and financial rules before deployment or data sharing.
 • Expose documented, authorized partner APIs only after tenant-isolation tests.
 • Publish clear study methods so research partners can evaluate real-world outcomes.

19 | Project definition of done
A real farmer can join an approved organization, publish a legitimate harvest, receive an
explainable urgency assessment and buyer matches, accept an authorized offer with atomic
stock reservation, arrange fulfillment, record verified receipt and reconcile the resulting sale,
unsold quantity and losses—without manual database edits. A coordinator can inspect each
state transition. Unauthorized actors cannot alter it. Only verified outcomes appear as
achieved impact.
Recommended engineering order: (1) record system, (2) consistent commerce workflow, (3) transparent
intelligence, (4) provider coordination, (5) measurement, (6) advanced AI. The system should be credible before
it is complex.
